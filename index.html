1. One-Way Trip CO₂
CO2_trip (kg) = (d * EF) / 1000

Where d is the distance in km and EF is the Indian ARAI/CEA emission factor in grams of CO₂ per passenger-km. Divided by 1,000 for kg conversion.

2. Commute Fuel Expense (₹)
Cost (₹) = (d / Mileage) * Fuel_Price

Computes real direct fuel expense using Indian retail prices (Petrol ₹96.72/L, Diesel ₹89.62/L, CNG ₹76.59/kg, or DISCOM electricity ₹7.50/kWh).

3. Tree Sequestration Absorption
Tree-Days = ceil(CO2_trip / (22 / 365))

Based on the standard 22 kg CO₂/tree/year absorption rate (~0.06027 kg/day), showing the biological offset requirement for your commute.

Emission factors are adapted from ARAI (Automotive Research Association of India), MoRTH BS6 Standards, India's Central Electricity Authority (CEA v19 Baseline: 0.72 kg CO₂/kWh), and DMRC / BEST transit reports.

Clean & CNG Alternatives

• Electric 2-Wheeler (Ola/Ather): 18 g/km (~3.2 kWh/100km)

Indian Mass Transit Systems

• Metro Rail (DMRC/MRTS): 32 g/km (high efficiency electric)
• CNG Auto-Rickshaw: 45 g/km (per seat load)
• State City Bus (DTC/BEST): 78 g/km (average 45 passenger load)

Why measure in "Tree-Days" instead of just trees? Planting 1 tree doesn't erase carbon instantly — carbon offset is a rate over time. A mature deciduous tree absorbs roughly 22 kilograms of CO₂ per 365 calendar days ($\approx 0.06027\text{ kg/day}$). If a trip generates $4.8\text{ kg of CO}_2$, it requires either 1 tree growing for 80 continuous days or a grove of 80 trees working for 1 full day to balance the release.

Q: Are Electric Vehicles really zero emission?

Answer for Professor: No, tailpipe (Tank-to-Wheel) is 0, but lifecycle (Well-to-Wheel) emissions depend on the grid power generation. In our dashboard, EV emissions reflect 53 g/km based on thermal-renewable average electricity generation.

Q: Why is Metro Rail so much cleaner than Buses?

Answer for Professor: Metro rail benefits from electric motor efficiency, steel-on-steel low rolling resistance, regenerative braking, and massive passenger density (hundreds of commuters per rake).

Q: How does passenger carpooling affect figures?

Answer for Professor: Carbon is inversely proportional to occupancy. Sharing a petrol car with 4 passengers drops per-person emissions from 192 g/km to 48 g/km, matching public transit!
